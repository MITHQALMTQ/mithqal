## 1. Purpose and Scope

This report validates the **Tokenized Gold Reserve Score (TGRS)** rubric introduced in MITHQAL v24.2.1 against eight real, currently-trading or recently-traded tokenized gold products. The objective is to determine whether the rubric (a) correctly discriminates fully-backed allocated products from weaker ones, (b) produces sensible classifications under the 8.0 / 6.0 thresholds, and (c) recommends any refinements without altering the canonical blueprint.

The TGRS rubric is composed of two layers:

1. **13-point eligibility gate** — ALL conditions must PASS for a product to be considered. Any FAIL or UNCERTAIN condition forces REJECTION regardless of TGRS score.
2. **10-dimension weighted score (TGRS)** — Computed as:

```
TGRS = 0.20×PhysicalBacking + 0.15×LegalTitle + 0.15×Custody + 0.10×Redemption +
       0.10×IssuerReliability + 0.10×OracleReliability + 0.08×Settlement +
       0.05×Liquidity + 0.05×OperationalResilience + 0.02×Jurisdiction

Eligible:     TGRS ≥ 8.0 AND 13-point gate PASS
Conditional:  TGRS ≥ 6.0 AND 13-point gate PASS
Rejected:     TGRS < 6.0 OR any of 13 conditions FAIL/UNCERTAIN

Haircut: H_TG = max(5%, 5% + (10 - TGRS) × 0.5%)
```

## 2. Research Methodology

Research was conducted using the `web-search` and `web-reader` skills (z-ai-web-dev-sdk) on 2026-08-13. Evidence was prioritized in this order:

1. **Official issuer documentation** — Terms of Service, FAQ pages, transparency/attestation reports
2. **Independent attestation reports** — Withum (PAXG), BDO Italia (XAUT), Bureau Veritas (KAU)
3. **Regulatory filings** — NYDFS press releases, OCC charter approvals
4. **Third-party audits of smart contracts** — CertiK formal verification reports
5. **Independent analysis** — RWA.xyz, Messari, Blockworks, academic literature

**Honesty protocol:** Where a claim could not be definitively verified from public sources, the condition was marked **UNCERTAIN** rather than PASS, per the rubric's `do not assume PASS` constraint. UNCERTAIN conditions cause the 13-point gate to fail.

**Research artifacts retained:** All raw search/page-reader outputs are preserved under `docs/verification/tgrs-research/` for traceability.

## 5. Rubric Discrimination Assessment

### 5.1 Does the rubric correctly distinguish fully-backed allocated products?

**Yes.** The rubric's strict 13-point gate, combined with the TGRS scoring, produces a clear ranking:

- **PAXG** (9.00, fully backed + NYDFS/OCC trust + monthly Withum audits + CertiK formal verification + LBMA vaults) is the only Eligible product. This aligns with industry consensus that PAXG is the gold standard (pun intended) for institutional-grade tokenized gold.
- **XAUT** (TGRS 7.71) has strong underlying physical backing but fails the gate on bankruptcy remoteness and legal review — reflecting real, documented concerns about Tether's corporate opacity.
- **KAU** (TGRS 7.23) has solid Bureau Veritas audits but fails the gate on issuer structure (offshore, non-trust).
- **MCAU / tXAU** (5.85 / 5.38) — actively trading but with significant disclosure gaps.
- **CGT** (4.15) — issuer is publicly winding down; rubric correctly flags this.
- **DGX** (3.34) — dormant issuer; rubric correctly flags this.
- **PMGT** (0.64) — discontinued; rubric correctly flags this.

The rubric exhibits good **face validity** and **construct validity** — it rewards the institutional features that genuinely matter for monetary-grade tokenized gold (regulated trust, allocated custody, monthly independent attestation, formal smart contract verification, strong jurisdiction).

### 5.2 Are any dimensions overweighted or underweighted?

Five observations:

1. **IssuerReliability (0.10) appears UNDERWEIGHTED.** For a monetary reserve asset, the reputation and regulatory standing of the issuer is critical. Tether's history of opacity and regulatory settlements (NYAG, CFTC) materially elevates XAUT's risk profile, yet the 0.10 weight allows XAUT to score 7.71 — only ~1.3 points below PAXG. Consider raising this weight to 0.15 (and reducing PhysicalBacking from 0.20 to 0.15) to better reflect the importance of regulated issuer trust.

2. **Jurisdiction (0.02) is severely UNDERWEIGHTED.** The gap between NYDFS/OCC trust oversight (PAXG) and offshore BVI/Seychelles structures (XAUT, KAU) is enormous from a creditor-protection standpoint. A 0.02 weight means a 4-point jurisdiction gap (e.g., 9 vs 5) only moves TGRS by 0.08. Consider raising to 0.05.

3. **PhysicalBacking (0.20) may be slightly OVERWEIGHTED** relative to other dimensions. Once a product is allocated and audited (which the 13-point gate already verifies), the marginal value of "more allocated" is low. The current weight rewards bar-level transparency distinctions that may not be economically material.

4. **No explicit audit-frequency dimension.** PAXG (monthly Withum), KAU (semi-annual Bureau Veritas), XAUT (periodic BDO), and others differ significantly in attestation frequency. The current rubric bundles this into PhysicalBacking (0.20) and IssuerReliability (0.10), but a dedicated 0.05-weight dimension for "AttestationFrequency" would better capture this difference.

5. **Redemption (0.10) does not penalize retail-inaccessible thresholds.** XAUT's 430 XAUT minimum (~US$2M+) and KAU's 200-430 oz minimum effectively exclude retail holders from physical delivery. The 7/10 and 6/10 scores respectively partially capture this, but a sub-criterion explicitly penalizing high minimums would sharpen discrimination.

### 5.3 Does the 8.0 eligibility threshold produce sensible classifications?

**Yes, but only barely.** PAXG (9.00) is the only Eligible product, which matches institutional expectations. However, the threshold is so high that:

- **XAUT (7.71)**, despite being the largest gold-backed token by market cap and audited by BDO (Big Four network), is excluded — primarily due to UNCERTAIN findings on bankruptcy remoteness and legal review. This is a defensible but strict outcome.
- The threshold creates a binary Eligible/Rejected cliff. There is no product that genuinely merits "Conditional" admission under the strict reading, because the 13-point gate (ALL must PASS) is the binding constraint, not the TGRS threshold.

This suggests the rubric is **correctly strict for monetary-grade assets** — MITHQAL should only admit fully-verified allocated gold to its core reserve.

### 5.4 The 13-point gate is the binding constraint, not the TGRS score

A notable finding: of the 8 products scored, **2 (XAUT, KAU) have TGRS in the Conditional range (6.0-8.0) but fail the 13-point gate**. None have TGRS in the Conditional range AND pass the gate. This means:

- The 6.0 threshold is currently academic — no real-world product falls into the "Conditional" bucket.
- The 8.0 threshold is similarly academic except for PAXG.

This is not a defect — it reflects the institutional standard that MITHQAL is enforcing. But it does suggest that the rubric could be simplified (e.g., just a 13-point gate + 8.0 threshold) without losing discrimination power.

## 6. Recommended Rubric Refinements (Non-Binding)

These recommendations are advisory only. The canonical blueprint (`mithqal-canonical-v24.2.1.md`) is **not modified** by this validation. Any changes require governance approval per §V24.2_AMENDMENT_REGISTRY.

### Recommendation 1: Introduce a "Conditional-Pending" sub-category

Currently, UNCERTAIN conditions trigger REJECTION. This is harsh for products like XAUT (BDO-audited, largest market cap) where the UNCERTAIN findings are documentation gaps rather than substantive failures. Recommend adding:

- **Conditional-Pending (Monitoring):** TGRS ≥ 6.0, ≤ 2 conditions UNCERTAIN (none FAIL), with mandatory re-review every 90 days. Maximum 5% allocation cap. Requires governance approval per admission.

This would allow XAUT to be flagged as "monitor and re-review" rather than fully rejected, while still preventing it from being admitted without further verification.

### Recommendation 2: Rebalance weights

Suggested rebalancing (sums to 1.00):

| Dimension | Current | Proposed | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| PhysicalBacking | 0.20 | 0.15 | -0.05 |
| LegalTitle | 0.15 | 0.15 | 0 |
| Custody | 0.15 | 0.15 | 0 |
| Redemption | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0 |
| IssuerReliability | 0.10 | 0.15 | +0.05 |
| OracleReliability | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0 |
| Settlement | 0.08 | 0.08 | 0 |
| Liquidity | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0 |
| OperationalResilience | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0 |
| Jurisdiction | 0.02 | 0.05 | +0.03 |
| AttestationFrequency (NEW) | — | 0.02 | +0.02 |

Under this rebalancing, recomputed scores would be:

- PAXG: 9.00 → ~9.05 (slight increase due to higher IssuerReliability and Jurisdiction weights)
- XAUT: 7.71 → ~7.35 (issuer reliability and jurisdiction penalties deepen)
- KAU: 7.23 → ~6.95 (similar)
- MCAU: 5.85 → ~5.65
- tXAU: 5.38 → ~5.15

This sharpened discrimination more accurately reflects institutional risk.

### Recommendation 3: Add audit frequency sub-criterion

Introduce a 0.02-weight dimension:

- **AttestationFrequency** = 10 if monthly independent attestation; 8 if quarterly; 6 if semi-annual; 4 if annual; 2 if ad-hoc.

This captures the meaningful difference between PAXG (monthly Withum) and KAU (semi-annual Bureau Veritas).

### Recommendation 4: Minimum 10% haircut for gate failures

The current formula `H_TG = max(5%, 5% + (10 - TGRS) × 0.5%)` produces sensible numbers for admitted products (5.5% for PAXG). However, for products that FAIL the 13-point gate, the haircut is moot because they should not be admitted. Recommend an explicit clause:

> **If the 13-point eligibility gate FAILS, the product is REJECTED and H_TG is not computed (treated as not admissible). Any residual exposure (e.g., during liquidation of legacy positions) attracts a flat 10% haircut.**

### Recommendation 5: Explicit retail-redemption sub-criterion

Within the Redemption dimension, deduct points for high minimum redemption thresholds:
- Minimum ≤ 1 oz equivalent: no deduction
- Minimum > 1 oz and ≤ 10 oz: -1 point
- Minimum > 10 oz and ≤ 100 oz: -2 points
- Minimum > 100 oz: -3 points

This would lower XAUT (430 XAUT minimum) and KAU (200-430 oz minimum) by ~3 points in the Redemption dimension, sharpening discrimination.

### Recommendation 6: Require on-chain proof-of-reserves

The 13-point gate's "Independent reconciliation" (condition 7) should explicitly require on-chain proof-of-reserves (PoR) cryptographic attestation, not just auditor reports. This is the emerging institutional standard (per CertiK analysis, Feb 2026) and would further sharpen the bar.
