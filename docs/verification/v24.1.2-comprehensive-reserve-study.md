## 1.2 Why Gold at 15%?

**Equation for optimal gold weight:**
```
W_gold* = argmin [ CVaR_99(Portfolio) ] subject to:
  LCR ≥ 1.0
  Stress-RR ≥ 100%
  Liquidity cost ≤ threshold
  W_gold ∈ [12%, 18%]
```

**Rationale:**
- At 15% weight, a 50% gold crash costs only ~8.7 percentage points of RR (from 110% to ~101%)

**Specialist concern:** Gold at 15% means MITHQAL is NOT "gold-backed" — it is "gold-anchored." The distinction is critical: gold provides strategic reference, not redemption guarantee.

**Rationale:**
- Silver has 30% annual volatility (2× gold) — too volatile for higher allocation
- Silver market is thinner (20 bps execution cost vs 10 bps for gold)
- Silver provides diversification benefit when gold-silver correlation breaks
- At 5% weight, a 50% silver crash costs only ~1.2 percentage points of RR
- BRI weight is 90% gold / 10% silver — silver is secondary by design

- **Problem:** When all assets fall simultaneously (correlation → 1), the 120% strategic buffer is exactly a 20% cushion — no margin
- **Affected scenarios:** rho +50%/+75%/→1
- **Impact:** RR drops to 88-99%
- **Why it fails:** The buffer is designed for idiosyncratic shocks, not systematic shocks. Diversification (the primary systematic defense) fails when correlations break.

### Root Cause 3: COMBINED/COMPOSITE SHOCKS (3 failures)
- **Problem:** When multiple shocks occur simultaneously (gold + FX, or redemption + market stress), the combined impact exceeds single-shock resilience
- **Affected scenarios:** Composite gold+FX, USD -50%, redemption + stress
- **Impact:** RR drops to 96-99%
- **Why it fails:** The system is calibrated for single-factor shocks. Multi-factor stress compounds non-linearly.
