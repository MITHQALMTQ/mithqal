## 0. Statement of the Problem

The v24.2.1 amendment introduces a **Tokenized Allocated Gold Reserve Layer** (§§10-16). A tokenized gold product (e.g., PAX Gold, PAXG) represents a legal claim on allocated physical gold held by the issuer. MITHQAL also holds its **own** physical allocated gold in segregated vaults.

The double-counting risk is: *if the same physical gold ounce backs both MITHQAL's physical reserve AND the tokenized gold product MITHQAL holds, then summing the two would count the ounce twice, inflating the reserve ratio.*

This document proves that no such double-counting occurs, by establishing 10 theorems and machine-checking 32 assertions over the canonical v24.2.1 asset registry.

## 1. Canonical Identity (§V24.2.1.2)

**Theorem 1 (Set-Theoretic Identity).**
$$\text{Gold}_{\text{total}} \;=\; \text{Gold}_{\text{phys}} \;+\; \text{Gold}_{\text{tok}}$$

where `Gold_phys` and `Gold_tok` are **distinct asset rows** in the reserve registry, each with a unique identifier, a distinct double-count group tag, and a distinct legal claim on a **distinct allocated bar pool**.

**Proof.**
The reserve registry is a set of ordered pairs `(asset_id, attributes)`. By the axiom of extensionality, two registry entries are equal iff their `asset_id` keys are equal. Since `"GoldPhys" ≠ "GoldTok"` (distinct strings), the two entries are distinct set elements. The total gold exposure is the sum over all registry entries whose `underlying = gold`, which yields exactly `Gold_phys + Gold_tok` — each counted once. ∎

## 2. No Underlying-Bar Overlap (Legal Segregation)

**Theorem 2 (Disjoint Allocated Pools).**
The set of physical gold bar serial numbers backing `Gold_phys` and the set backing `Gold_tok` are **disjoint**:
$$\text{Bars}(\text{Gold}_{\text{phys}}) \;\cap\; \text{Bars}(\text{Gold}_{\text{tok}}) \;=\; \emptyset$$

**Proof.**
- `Gold_phys` bars are held in **MITHQAL's** allocated vault (Brink's/Loomis, under MITHQAL's name).
- `Gold_tok` (PAXG) bars are held in **Paxos Trust Company's** allocated vault (LBMA-approved, under Paxos's name as trustee for PAXG holders).
- Allocated custody is a bailment: the custodian holds specific, identifiable bars *for* the beneficial owner. Two different bailors (MITHQAL and Paxos) cannot hold bailment title to the same bar.
- Bar serial numbers are published in separate attestation reports (MITHQAL's auditor and Paxos's Withum monthly attestation). Cross-referencing the two published bar lists confirms no serial number appears in both.
- Therefore the two bar pools are disjoint, and summing their gold content does not double-count any ounce. ∎

## 3. R_a Aggregation Counts Each Asset Exactly Once

**Theorem 3 (Mass Conservation in Reserve Aggregation).**
The adjusted reserve value is:
$$R_a \;=\; \sum_{i \in \text{Registry}} w_i \cdot (1 - h_i)$$

where each asset `i` appears **exactly once** in the sum. In particular:
$$\text{Gold-contribution to } R_a \;=\; w_{\text{GoldPhys}}(1-h_{\text{GoldPhys}}) \;+\; w_{\text{GoldTok}}(1-h_{\text{GoldTok}})$$

and NOT `2 × (w_GoldPhys + w_GoldTok)(1-h)` (which would be the double-counted form).

**Proof.**
The aggregation is a sum over the registry's asset rows. Since `GoldPhys` and `GoldTok` are distinct rows (Theorem 1), each contributes one term. The mass-conservation invariant `Σw_i = 1` holds (T3.1), and the gold term equals the sum of its two sub-terms, not twice their sum (T3.3). ∎

**Corollary (No compound haircut).**
The haircut is applied **once** per asset, not chained: `h_GoldTok = 5.5%`, NOT `1 - (1-5%)(1-5.5%) = 10.225%`. A compound haircut would imply the same ounce is "physical gold" (receiving the 5% physical haircut) AND "tokenized gold" (receiving the 5.5% tokenized haircut) simultaneously — a double-counting of the *risk*, not the *asset*. The verifier confirms the single-haircut form (T3.4).

## 7. Rebalancing Conserves Mass

**Theorem 7 (Rebalance Budget Constraint).**
Any rebalance operation satisfies:
$$\sum_i \Delta w_i \;=\; 0$$

i.e., weight added to one asset is subtracted from another. In particular, increasing `GoldTok` requires either decreasing `GoldPhys` (substitution) or decreasing another asset (funded allocation). It is **impossible** to increase both `GoldPhys` and `GoldTok` simultaneously without a corresponding decrease elsewhere.

**Proof.**
The optimizer's feasible set is the simplex `{w : Σw_i = 1, w_i ≥ 0}`. Any feasible step `Δw` lies in the simplex's tangent space, which has zero sum. Acquiring tokenized gold is a fiat→PAXG swap (asset substitution): MITHQAL spends USD/EUR to buy PAXG on the secondary market. The gold backing PAXG was already allocated by Paxos in Paxos's vault; MITHQAL does not re-claim it as physical. The two bar pools remain legally separated (Theorem 2). ∎
