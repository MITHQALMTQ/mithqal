## 1. Executive Summary

This backtest evaluates whether silver (3% strategic weight in v24.2 baseline) earns
its place in the MITHQAL reserve portfolio under the v24.2.1 conditional silver policy,
using REAL historical monthly prices for gold, silver, and 11 fiat currencies over the
68-month period from January 2020 through August 2025.

**The honest finding: silver is a BORDERLINE diversifier whose admission depends
critically on the LCR-normalization philosophy.** Two defensible interpretations of
the SDC_Ag formula produce opposite verdicts, each with strong statistical confidence:

| LCR normalization | Mean SDC_Ag (bp) | t-stat | 95% CI (bp) | Months admit silver | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary — Shortfall** (LCR above 1.5 has marginal value 0) | **+77.27** | +7.09 | [+56, +98] | **42 / 57 (73.7 %)** | **SILVER ADMITTED** |
| **Sensitivity — Proportional** (every LCR unit = 100 bp) | **−205.60** | −13.03 | [−236, −175] | 3 / 57 (5.3 %) | SILVER = 0% (VALID) |

**Bottom-line recommendation for v24.2.1 policy:** The conservative default of
**Silver = 0 %** is defensible and is the safer policy choice given that:
1. Silver HURTS in stress periods (COVID-2020, 2022 inflation drawdown) under both views.
2. The "primary" (shortfall-LCR) view that admits silver relies on treating LCR above
   the regulatory minimum as having zero marginal value — a defensible but optimistic
   regulatory interpretation.
3. Portfolio C (dynamic SDC_Ag-driven silver) **underperforms** both static A and B
   in mean RR (103.83 % vs 105.21 % vs 104.22 %) — the rolling SDC_Ag signal lags
   silver's actual diversification timing.

Conditional admission to 3 % is **also defensible** under the shortfall-LCR view,
particularly during strong precious-metals rallies (e.g., 2024-2025). But the
v24.2.1 design philosophy — "Silver = 0 % is a VALID outcome" — is the more
conservative reading of the same evidence.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Portfolio construction

Three portfolios are simulated on identical historical data:

| Portfolio | Description | Silver | Sovereign (T-bills) | Gold | Fiat | Digital |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | v24.2 baseline (silver mandatory) | 3.0 % | 7.5 % | 15.0 % | 70.5 % | 3.5 % |
| **B** | v24.2.1 default (silver = 0 %) | 0.0 % | 10.5 % | 15.0 % | 70.5 % | 3.5 % |
| **C** | v24.2.1 conditional (dynamic SDC_Ag) | 0 % or 3 % | 10.5 % or 7.5 % | 15.0 % | 70.5 % | 3.5 % |

The 3 % delta between A and B is moved between Silver and Sovereign (T-bills),
holding all other weights constant. This isolates the silver effect cleanly.

**Fiat basket (70.5 %):** USD 21.0 %, EUR 19.5 %, CHF 6.0 %, JPY 6.0 %, GBP 5.0 %,
SGD 4.0 %, AED 3.0 %, SAR 3.0 %, CNY 2.0 %, CAD 0.5 %, AUD 0.5 % — from v24.2
portfolio table.

**Digital (3.5 %):** USDC 2.0 %, USDP 0.5 %, EURC 0.5 %, BUIDL 0.5 %.

**Starting portfolio value:** $64.8 M (= $54 M liability × 1.20 RR target).
Liability held constant at $54 M (no minting/redeeming during backtest).

### 2.2 Haircuts, stress coefficients, HQLA factors

From v24.2.1 blueprint §3.4, §3.6, §7:

| Asset | Haircut H | Stress coeff S | HQLA factor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gold (allocated physical) | 5 % | 0.85 | 0.00 (Bullion Protection Rule) |
| Silver (allocated physical) | 7 % | 0.80 | 0.00 |
| Cash (USD, AED, SAR) | 0 % | 0.95 | 1.00 |
| Cash (other FX) | 2 % | 0.90 | 0.80 – 0.85 |
| Sovereign T-bills | 2 % | 0.90 | 1.00 |
| Stablecoins (USDC/USDP/EURC) | 2 % | 0.80 | 0.60 |
| BUIDL (tokenized T-bills) | 2 % | 0.90 | 0.85 |

Per §7 of the blueprint, gold and silver are EXCLUDED from base HQLA — they are not
counted as immediately realizable liquidity. This is the structural source of
silver's LCR drag relative to sovereign T-bills.

### 2.3 Monthly NAV reconstruction

For each month t (t = 1 … 68):

1. Rebalance to target weights (monthly, cost-free — execution cost applied only
   via the SDC_Ag net_cost term).
2. Apply asset-specific monthly returns:
   - Gold, Silver, FX (EUR/JPY/GBP/CHF/AUD/CAD/SGD/CNY/AED/SAR): historical price ratio.
   - USD (numeraire): 0 % monthly return (it's the unit of account).
   - Sovereign T-bills: constant 3.0 %/year yield (≈ 2020-2025 average 3-month T-bill).
   - Stablecoins (USDC/USDP/EURC): 4.0 %/year (≈ 2023-2024 reserve yield).
   - BUIDL: 4.5 %/year.
3. Compute adjusted reserve R_a = Σ (asset_value × (1 − H_a)).
4. Compute stressed reserve R_stress = Σ (asset_value × (1 − H_a) × S_a).
5. RR = R_a / LIABILITY × 100. StressRR = R_stress / LIABILITY × 100.
6. HQLA = Σ (asset_value × HQLA_factor). LCR = HQLA / (monthly_outflows), where
   monthly_outflows = LIABILITY × 0.10 / 12 (10 % annual stress outflow, per
   v24.2 Monte Carlo engine).
7. Loss = max(0, BASELINE_RA − R_a), where BASELINE_RA = $64.8 M (RR = 120 % target).

### 2.4 Risk metrics

- **CVaR_99**: Conditional Value-at-Risk at 99 % confidence. Computed empirically
  as the mean of the worst 1 % of monthly losses (with 68 observations, this is
  effectively the worst monthly loss plus interpolation).
- **VaR_95, VaR_99**: Empirical percentiles of monthly losses.
- **P(RR < 100 %)**: fraction of months where the reserve ratio fell below the
  solvency threshold.
- **P(StressRR < 100 %)**: same for stressed reserve ratio.

### 2.5 SDC_Ag computation

```
SDC_Ag = net_resilience_gain − net_cost
net_resilience_gain = CVaR_improvement + StressRR_improvement + LCR_improvement
net_cost = execution_cost + custody_cost + volatility_penalty + liquidity_penalty
```

All quantities converted to **basis-points (bp) of liability** for consistent units.

**Sign convention:** We define each "improvement" term as POSITIVE when silver
provides benefit:
- CVaR_improvement = (CVaR_B − CVaR_A) / LIABILITY × 10000 bp  (positive when silver reduces tail losses)
- StressRR_improvement = (StressRR_A − StressRR_B) × 100 bp  (positive when silver improves StressRR; 1 % StressRR = 100 bp of liability)
- LCR_improvement = (LCR_A − LCR_B) × scale  (positive when silver improves LCR)

> **Note on task-spec sign convention:** The task's parenthesized formula
> `(CVaR_A − CVaR_B) + (StressRR_B − StressRR_A) + (LCR_B − LCR_A)`
> has the opposite sign on all three terms relative to natural "improvement"
> semantics. We interpret the author's intent as "improvement" (positive = helps)
> and document this deviation. Under our convention, SDC_Ag > 0 ⟹ silver helps.

**LCR normalization — the critical fork:** Silver is structurally non-HQLA
(per §7 Bullion Protection Rule). Adding silver at 3 % weight displaces 3 %
of Sovereign T-bills (HQLA factor 1.0) from the portfolio. This creates a
structural LCR drag of ~3 LCR units (LCR_A ≈ 93 vs LCR_B ≈ 96).

The magnitude of this LCR drag in bp depends on the marginal value of additional
HQLA buffer:

- **Primary (Shortfall) view:** Following standard regulatory capital practice,
  the marginal value of additional HQLA buffer is ~0 when LCR is well above the
  regulatory minimum (we use threshold 1.5 as a conservative buffer above the
  Basel III minimum of 1.0). Below the threshold, each unit of LCR shortfall
  = 1000 bp of liability (regulatory-breach scale). With LCR ~93-96 (both far
  above 1.5), **LCR_improvement = 0 bp**.
- **Sensitivity (Proportional) view:** Every unit of LCR diff is worth
  LCR_BP_PER_UNIT_MARGINAL = 100 bp, regardless of level. This treats LCR
  buffer as having constant marginal value. With LCR_A − LCR_B ≈ −2.86,
  **LCR_improvement ≈ −286 bp** (silver hurts).

Both views are defensible. We report both for transparency.

### 2.6 Net cost components (bp/year, applied to silver portion only)

| Component | Formula | Value at w_silver = 3 % |
|---|---|---|
| Execution cost | 30 bp round-trip × w × 4 (quarterly rebalance) | 3.60 bp/yr |
| Custody cost | 15 bp/yr × w | 0.45 bp/yr |
| Volatility penalty | w × (σ_silver − σ_gold) × 10000 (Sharpe = 1.0) | 45.00 bp/yr |
| Liquidity penalty | w × bid_ask (30 bp) × 4 rebalances × 10000 | 3.60 bp/yr |
| **Total net cost** | | **52.65 bp/yr** |

### 2.7 Portfolio C — dynamic silver admission

At end of month t, look back 12 months (SDC_TRAILING_WINDOW = 12). Compute
rolling SDC_Ag over that window. If SDC_Ag > 0, set silver = 3 % for month t+1;
else silver = 0 %. For the first 12 months (no history), use the v24.2.1
conservative default of 0 %.

The decision rule uses the **primary** (shortfall-LCR) SDC_Ag, consistent with
the standard regulatory interpretation.

### 2.8 Statistical significance

Bootstrap test (10,000 resamples, seed = 42) of the null hypothesis
H₀: mean rolling SDC_Ag ≤ 0 vs H₁: mean > 0. We report the bootstrap 95 %
confidence interval and one-sided p-value.

## 9. VERDICT

### 9.1 Honest reading of the evidence

The SDC_Ag decision rule, applied to 68 months of real historical data, produces
a **borderline** result that depends on the LCR normalization philosophy:

1. **If LCR above the regulatory minimum has zero marginal value** (standard
   regulatory practice; "Shortfall" view): **SILVER ADMITTED** with strong
   statistical confidence (mean +77 bp, t = +7.1, 95 % CI [+56, +98], 74 % of
   months admit). The CVaR reduction (+133 bp) and StressRR improvement (+60 bp)
   together exceed the 53 bp/year net cost.

2. **If every LCR unit has constant marginal value** (pessimistic;
   "Proportional" view): **SILVER = 0 %** with strong statistical confidence
   (mean −206 bp, t = −13.0, 95 % CI [−236, −175], 5 % of months admit). The
   structural LCR drag (−285 bp) overwhelms the resilience gains.

3. **In acute stress periods (COVID-2020, 2022 inflation):** Silver HURTS under
   BOTH views — it amplified drawdowns rather than dampening them. A genuine
   diversifier should help in stress, not hurt.

4. **Portfolio C (dynamic) underperforms both static portfolios** in mean RR
   (103.83 % vs 105.21 % vs 104.22 %). The rolling SDC_Ag signal lags silver's
   actual diversification timing — admission decisions are made on stale data.

### 9.2 Final verdict

> **SILVER = 0 % (VALID POLICY RESULT) — Recommended**

The v24.2.1 conservative default of Silver = 0 % is the **safer and more
defensible** policy choice, for the following reasons:

1. **Silver is a borderline diversifier at best.** Its CVaR and StressRR
   improvements (+192 bp combined) are modest relative to its structural LCR
   cost (−285 bp in the proportional view, or 0 in the shortfall view).

2. **Silver HURTS in acute stress**, when diversification is most needed.
   COVID-2020 and 2022 inflation drawdowns both show silver amplifying losses.

3. **The SDC_Ag signal is too laggy to be useful in real-time.** Portfolio C
   (dynamic) underperforms Portfolio B (static 0 %) by 39 bp in mean RR.

4. **The proportional-LCR sensitivity view** — which treats every unit of LCR
   buffer as having constant marginal value — rejects silver with t = −13.0.
   This is a defensible conservative interpretation.

5. **The shortfall-LCR view that admits silver** relies on the assumption
   that LCR buffer above 1.5 has zero marginal value. While standard in
   regulatory practice, this is optimistic and may not hold under severe
   stress where additional HQLA buffer provides option value.

### 9.3 Conditional admission — when is it justified?

Under the primary (shortfall-LCR) view, silver is admitted in 42 / 57 months
(74 %). The clearest case for admission is during sustained precious-metals
rallies (e.g., 2024-2025), where silver provides +93 to +242 bp of SDC_Ag
benefit. If governance wishes to retain the conditional 0-3 % band (rather
than remove silver entirely), the SDC_Ag decision rule should:
- Use a longer trailing window (24 months instead of 12) to reduce signal lag.
- Apply a positive-admission threshold (e.g., SDC_Ag > +50 bp) to avoid
  borderline admissions that flip month-to-month.
- Explicitly reject silver during identified stress regimes (per §33 state
  machine: DEFENSIVE / STRESS / EMERGENCY).
