# Geographic Custody Strategy

**Document Date:** 2026-08-09
**Author:** Chief Enterprise Architect / Institutional Infrastructure Architect (acting in concert)
**Status:** CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT PROPOSAL — pending Council ratification
**Authority:** §8 of `docs/blueprint/custody-framework-v2.md`

---

## Purpose

This document defines MITHQAL's geographic diversification strategy for reserve custody. It implements the constitutional requirement (§Article XVII §12) that **no single jurisdiction may hold more than 30% of total reserves**, and the operational target that **at least 3 jurisdictions** are used, with the **top 2 jurisdictions holding no more than 60% combined**.

The strategy pursues **five regions** to eliminate single-jurisdiction concentration risk and provide institutional-grade redundancy across the world's major financial centers.

---

## Strategic Principles

1. **No single jurisdiction >30% of total reserves** (constitutional binding cap, §Article XVII §12)
2. **At least 3 jurisdictions**, with top 2 ≤60% combined (constitutional requirement)
3. **Tier 3 bullion special rule**: held in vaults across three jurisdictions, with no single jurisdiction holding more than 8% of total reserves
4. **Tier 1 official-sector custody is the highest-trust target where legally available** — pursued as a long-term objective, not assumed
5. **Tier 2 regulated bank custody is the practical backbone** — the primary operational route
6. **Tier 3 specialized vault custody provides geographic redundancy** — especially for physical bullion
7. **No custodian is designated without due diligence and contractual agreement**
8. **No central bank is represented as a custodian until it actually agrees to the role**

### 🇺🇸 United States

**Strategic importance:** The US is the world's largest financial market and home to the Federal Reserve System, the largest gold vault (NY Fed), and major institutional custodians.

| Layer | Target | Practical Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Official-Sector)** | Federal Reserve Bank of New York | — | The NY Fed is exceptionally strong from a credibility perspective, but it is **not realistic to assume MITHQAL can simply open an account there**. Its published policy says private-sector entities cannot store gold in its vault. Long-term objective only. |
| **Tier 2 (Regulated Bank)** | J.P. Morgan | BNY Mellon, State Street | JPMorgan has a large global custody infrastructure and reports custody coverage across 50 markets. It is an established gold custodian; SEC-filed documentation identifies JPMorgan as custodian for allocated gold bullion. |
| **Tier 3 (Specialized Vault)** | Brink's (US vaults) | Loomis (US vaults) | Both are LBMA-registered global vault operators with US infrastructure. |

**MITHQAL target:** NY Fed (long-term official possibility) → JPMorgan / equivalent institutional custodian (practical route)

**Jurisdictional cap:** 30% of total reserves maximum (constitutional). Operational target: ≤25%.

### 🇬🇧 Europe / London

**Strategic importance:** London is the world's principal gold-vaulting location. The Bank of England currently holds approximately 400,000 gold bars and provides custody for the UK and other central banks. The 2026 World Gold Council central-bank survey found the Bank of England remained the most popular gold-vaulting location, cited by 57% of respondents.

| Layer | Target | Practical Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Official-Sector)** | Bank of England | BIS (Basel) | The Bank of England primarily provides gold accounts to central banks, but can consider certain commercial firms under specific criteria. Long-term objective. |
| **Tier 2 (Regulated Bank)** | HSBC | JPMorgan (London branch) | HSBC describes itself as one of the world's largest precious-metals custodians and provides trading, financing, vaulting, and physical-metal services across major markets including London, New York, and Hong Kong. |
| **Tier 3 (Specialized Vault)** | Independent London vaults | Brink's (London), Loomis (London), Malca-Amit (London) | London has a deep ecosystem of independent LBMA-registered vault operators. |

**MITHQAL target:** Bank of England → HSBC / JPMorgan → independent vault diversification

**Jurisdictional cap:** 30% of total reserves maximum (constitutional). Operational target: ≤25%.

**Note:** This is where MITHQAL would put one of its most important gold custody relationships. The combination of Bank of England (official-sector, long-term), HSBC (regulated bank, practical), and independent London vaults (specialized) provides three layers of redundancy in a single jurisdiction.

### 🇦🇪 United Arab Emirates

**Strategic importance:** The UAE is strategically important for MITHQAL because of the project's intended institutional positioning. The Central Bank of the UAE's current rulebook expressly gives it powers including purchasing, selling, and taking gold and precious metals into custody, as well as taking assets into custody and maintaining accounts for banks, monetary authorities, and financial institutions. That makes CBUAE an important institutional relationship to explore.

| Layer | Target | Practical Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Official-Sector)** | Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) | — | CBUAE's rulebook expressly permits gold custody. Important for MITHQAL's institutional positioning. **But again: do not represent CBUAE as a MITHQAL custodian until CBUAE actually agrees to that role.** |
| **Tier 2 (Regulated Bank)** | Major UAE bank (e.g., Emirates NBD, FAB) | International bank with UAE presence | To be selected after due diligence. |
| **Tier 3 (Specialized Vault)** | Independent UAE vault | Brink's / Loomis UAE operations | Dubai is a growing precious-metals hub. |

**MITHQAL target:** CBUAE (strategic/official possibility) + major UAE bank (practical) + independent UAE vault (physical layer)

**Jurisdictional cap:** 30% of total reserves maximum (constitutional). Operational target: ≤25%.

**Practical UAE structure:** CBUAE — strategic/official custody possibility **plus** major UAE bank / institutional precious-metals custodian **plus** independent UAE vault. This gives MITHQAL redundancy rather than depending on one institution.

### 🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia

**Strategic importance:** Saudi Arabia is strategically important for MITHQAL's institutional positioning in the GCC region. The Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) is a significant monetary authority.

| Layer | Target | Practical Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Official-Sector)** | Saudi Central Bank (SAMA) | — | Subject to eligibility and regulatory approval. |
| **Tier 2 (Regulated Bank)** | Major Saudi institutional bank | International bank with KSA presence | **Do not put a particular Saudi commercial bank into the constitutional blueprint as "the custodian" yet.** Select the actual institution after due diligence and contractual negotiations. |
| **Tier 3 (Specialized Vault)** | Internationally recognized precious-metals vault operating under appropriate Saudi regulatory and security requirements | — | To be identified. |

**MITHQAL target:** SAMA (strategic, subject to eligibility) + major Saudi institutional bank (practical) + internationally recognized KSA vault (physical)

**Jurisdictional cap:** 30% of total reserves maximum (constitutional). Operational target: ≤25%.

### 🌏 Asia (Singapore + Hong Kong)

**Strategic importance:** For Asia, MITHQAL prioritizes Singapore and Hong Kong rather than trying to establish numerous small vault relationships. Both are major financial centers with strong financial-market infrastructure.

| Layer | Target | Practical Alternative | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Official-Sector)** | — (no central bank custody pursued in Asia initially) | — | Asian central banks (MAS, HKMA) are not initial Tier 1 targets. |
| **Tier 2 (Regulated Bank)** | Major international bank custody (HSBC Singapore, DBS, Standard Chartered) | JPMorgan (Singapore/HK) | Singapore and Hong Kong have deep international banking infrastructure. |
| **Tier 3 (Specialized Vault)** | Singapore institutional precious-metals vault (Malca-Amit Singapore) | Hong Kong vault (Brink's HK) | HSBC itself identifies Hong Kong, London, and New York among its principal precious-metals centres. |

**Institutional objective:**
- **Singapore**: major international bank custody + institutional precious-metals vault + strong financial-market infrastructure
- **Hong Kong**: HSBC / major bullion infrastructure + London–Hong Kong precious-metals connectivity

**MITHQAL target:** Major international bank custody (Singapore/HK) + specialized vault (Singapore/HK)

**Jurisdictional cap:** 30% of total reserves maximum (constitutional, per jurisdiction). Operational target: ≤25% per jurisdiction.

## Allocation Strategy

### Operational Targets (per jurisdiction)

| Jurisdiction | Target Share of Total Reserves | Maximum Share (Constitutional) | Primary Asset Classes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🇺🇸 United States | 20–25% | 30% | Cash (USD), Sovereign Securities (US Treasuries) |
| 🇬🇧 Europe / London | 20–25% | 30% | Physical Gold (allocated), Cash (GBP/EUR) |
| 🇦🇪 United Arab Emirates | 15–20% | 30% | Physical Gold (allocated), Cash (AED/USD), Sukuk |
| 🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia | 10–15% | 30% | Sukuk, Physical Gold (allocated) |
| 🌏 Asia (Singapore + Hong Kong) | 15–20% | 30% (per jurisdiction) | Cash (SGD/HKD/USD), Physical Silver (allocated) |

**Total:** 80–105% (the range allows for operational flexibility; the constitutional 100%+ reserve ratio is maintained separately).

### Tier 3 Bullion Special Allocation

For Tier 3 allocated physical bullion, the constitutional rule is stricter: **no single jurisdiction may hold more than 8% of total reserves** in bullion. This means bullion is spread across at least 4 jurisdictions (5–6 in practice).

| Jurisdiction | Target Bullion Share | Maximum Bullion Share | Vaults |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🇬🇧 London | 6–8% | 8% | Bank of England (Tier 1, long-term) + HSBC + independent London vaults |
| 🇦🇪 UAE | 5–7% | 8% | Independent UAE vault + CBUAE (Tier 1, long-term) |
| 🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia | 3–5% | 8% | Internationally recognized KSA vault |
| 🌏 Singapore | 4–6% | 8% | Malca-Amit Singapore |
| 🌏 Hong Kong | 3–5% | 8% | Brink's HK / HSBC HK |
| 🇺🇸 United States | 3–5% | 8% | Brink's US / Loomis US |

**Total bullion:** 24–36% of total reserves (within the Tier 3 target range of 10–30%).

## Rebalancing Rules

### Trigger Events

1. **Concentration breach** — a jurisdiction's share exceeds 30% (or 8% for bullion) due to market movement or custodian failure.
2. **Custodian failure** — a custodian becomes unavailable, compromised, or fails (per §Article XV Disaster Recovery Scenario #10).
3. **Regulatory event** — a change in law or regulation makes a custodian or jurisdiction unsuitable.
4. **Council decision** — the Constitutional Council directs a rebalance for institutional reasons.

### Rebalancing Timeline (per §Article XVII §12)

- **Concentration breach detected** → rebalancing initiated within 7 days
- **Rebalancing completed** within 30 days
- **Daily transparency disclosure** of concentration status throughout the rebalancing period

### Rebalancing Mechanism

1. **Primary:** Transfer assets from the over-concentrated custodian to an under-utilized custodian in a different jurisdiction.
2. **Secondary:** Engage a Tier 4 contingency custodian if no Tier 1/2/3 custodian has capacity.
3. **Emergency:** Liquidate a portion of the over-concentrated asset and re-allocate to a different asset class in a different jurisdiction (per the Constitutional Liquidation Order).

## Risk Considerations

### Jurisdiction-Specific Risks

| Jurisdiction | Key Risks | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 🇺🇸 United States | OFAC sanctions risk; regulatory enforcement risk; geopolitical risk | Sanctions screening (criterion 8); legal opinion on custody enforceability; diversification across other jurisdictions |
| 🇬🇧 Europe / London | Brexit-related regulatory divergence; FATF AML scrutiny | Legal opinion covering post-Brexit regulatory framework; AML/CFT compliance (criterion 7) |
| 🇦🇪 UAE | Regional geopolitical risk; evolving regulatory framework | Political risk assessment; CBUAE engagement; diversification across other jurisdictions |
| 🇸🇦 Saudi Arabia | Regional geopolitical risk; Sharia compliance requirements | Political risk assessment; SAMA engagement; Sharia Board review |
| 🌏 Singapore | Concentration of Asian custody in one hub | Hong Kong as secondary Asian jurisdiction; diversification across other regions |
| 🌏 Hong Kong | Geopolitical risk (US-China tensions); regulatory changes | Singapore as primary Asian jurisdiction; monitor regulatory developments |

### Concentration Risk

The constitutional 30% per-jurisdiction cap (and 8% per-jurisdiction bullion cap) is the primary mitigation for concentration risk. The operational targets (20–25% per jurisdiction) provide an additional buffer.

### Custodian Failure Risk

Per §Article XV Disaster Recovery Scenario #10, the failure of one primary custodian (holding up to 25% of reserves) triggers:
1. Activation of a Tier 4 contingency custodian (7-day RTO)
2. Transfer of assets to the contingency custodian
3. Daily transparency disclosure throughout the recovery period
4. Insurance recovery pursuit (if assets are lost or inaccessible)

## Implementation Phases

### Phase G-1 — Tier 2 + Tier 3 Engagement (Short-Term)
- Engage Tier 2 regulated bank custodians in the US (JPMorgan), UK (HSBC), and Singapore (DBS/Standard Chartered).
- Engage Tier 3 specialized vaults in London (Brink's/Loomis), Singapore (Malca-Amit), and the US (Brink's/Loomis).
- Reduce the current 52% single-custodian concentration (E048) below 25%.

### Phase G-2 — Geographic Expansion (Medium-Term)
- Engage Tier 2 custodians in the UAE (major UAE bank) and Saudi Arabia (major Saudi bank).
- Engage Tier 3 vaults in the UAE and Hong Kong.
- Achieve the 5-region diversification target.

### Phase G-3 — Official-Sector Engagement (Long-Term)
- Explore Tier 1 official-sector custody with CBUAE (most permissive rulebook).
- Explore Tier 1 custody with the Bank of England (for commercial firms under specific criteria).
- Engage the BIS for cross-border settlement infrastructure.
- Explore Tier 1 custody with SAMA (subject to eligibility).
- Long-term: explore NY Fed eligibility (requires qualifying as an official/institutional customer, which is not realistic in the near term).

### Phase G-4 — Public Reporting (Ongoing)
- Once custody agreements are executed and operational, publish the Reserve Custody Status indicator.
- No premature claims of official-sector custody.
- Public bar-level reserve reporting where legally and operationally appropriate.

## No Premature Claims

> **MITHQAL does not represent any central bank as a custodian until that institution actually agrees to the role.**

The dashboard may show:
- **"Reserve Custody: Pending"** — current status (no operational custody yet)
- **"Reserve Custody: Regulated Bank Custody"** — once Tier 2 agreements are executed
- **"Reserve Custody: Specialized Custody"** — once Tier 3 vault agreements are executed
- **"Reserve Custody: Official-Sector Custody"** — only when a Tier 1 central bank actually agrees and is independently documented
